## Topic 4: Linux PC-Based Router Performance

This is a **very important numerical topic** from your PPT. Your professor can ask direct numericals, modified numericals, conceptual questions, or "what if" scenarios.

---

# 1. Why study a Linux PC as a router?

A normal Linux PC can be configured to work as a router.

A simplified architecture is:

```text
Incoming Network
       ↓
    Input NIC
       ↓
    PCI Bus
       ↓
   Main Memory
       ↓
      CPU
       ↓
   Main Memory
       ↓
    PCI Bus
       ↓
   Output NIC
       ↓
Outgoing Network
```

The question is:

> **How fast can this PC-based router process and forward packets?**

The main performance metric is **throughput**.

---

# 2. What is Throughput?

Throughput tells us how much data the router can process and forward per unit time.

It can be expressed as:

### Packets per second

$$
\boxed{pps = packets/second}
$$

or

### Bits per second

$$
\boxed{bps = bits/second}
$$

For example:

$$
760\,Mbps
$$

means approximately 760 million bits can be processed per second.

---

# 3. Given Example from Your PPT

Your professor gives:

* PCI Bus bandwidth = **2 Gbps**
* CPU frequency = **2 GHz**
* Number of instructions = **200**
* Packet/header size = **40 bytes**

We need to calculate:

1. Time to transfer packet from NIC → memory
2. CPU processing time
3. Time to transfer memory → NIC
4. Total processing time
5. Router throughput

Let's derive it carefully.

---

# 4. Step 1 — NIC → Memory Transfer Time

Given:

$$
Packet\ size = 40\ bytes
$$

Convert bytes to bits:

$$
40\times8=320\ bits
$$

PCI bandwidth:

$$
2\,Gbps = 2\times10^9\ bits/sec
$$

Therefore:

$$
T_{transfer}=
\frac{320}{2\times10^9}
$$

$$
=160\times10^{-9}\ sec
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{T_{transfer}=160\,ns}
$$

---

# 5. Step 2 — CPU Processing Time

Given:

$$
Instructions=200
$$

CPU frequency:

$$
2\,GHz=2\times10^9\ cycles/sec
$$

Assuming one instruction takes one CPU cycle in this simplified model:

$$
T_{CPU}=
\frac{200}{2\times10^9}
$$

$$
=100\,ns
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{T_{CPU}=100\,ns}
$$

### Important assumption

The PPT is simplifying the calculation by treating the given instruction count as directly corresponding to CPU cycles.

In a real CPU, one instruction does not necessarily equal exactly one cycle.

---

# 6. Step 3 — Memory → Output NIC

The packet needs to travel back across the bus toward the output NIC.

Therefore, another:

$$
\boxed{160\,ns}
$$

is required.

---

# 7. Step 4 — Total Time

Total:

$$
T_{total}
=
T_{NIC\rightarrow Memory}
+
T_{CPU}
+
T_{Memory\rightarrow NIC}
$$

Therefore:

$$
T_{total}
=
160+100+160
$$

$$
\boxed{T_{total}=420\,ns}
$$

---

# 8. Step 5 — Calculate Throughput

Your PPT calculates throughput using:

$$
Throughput=
\frac{Packet\ Size}{Total\ Processing\ Time}
$$

Using:

$$
40\ bytes
$$

and:

$$
420\,ns
$$

Convert 40 bytes to bits:

$$
40\times8=320\ bits
$$

Therefore:

$$
Throughput=
\frac{320}{420\times10^{-9}}
$$

$$
\approx761.9\times10^6
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{Throughput\approx762\,Mbps}
$$

Your PPT rounds this to approximately:

$$
\boxed{760\,Mbps}
$$

---

# 9. The Important Observation

The PCI bus is:

$$
2\,Gbps
$$

But router throughput is only approximately:

$$
760\,Mbps
$$

So:

$$
760 < 2000
$$

The router cannot utilize the entire PCI bus bandwidth.

Why?

Because a significant amount of time is spent simply moving data.

From the total 420 ns:

$$
160+160=320\,ns
$$

is spent on data transfers.

Only:

$$
100\,ns
$$

is spent on CPU processing.

Therefore:

$$
\frac{320}{420}\approx76.2\%
$$

of the total time is spent transferring data in this simplified model.

---

# 10. Why is Throughput Approximately Half the Bus Bandwidth?

Your PPT makes the observation:

$$
Router\ Throughput\approx\frac{PCI\ Bus\ Bandwidth}{2}
$$

Why?

Because the packet effectively needs two bus transfers:

```text
Input NIC
   ↓
PCI Bus
   ↓
Memory
   ↓
CPU
   ↓
Memory
   ↓
PCI Bus
   ↓
Output NIC
```

So the same data crosses the bus approximately twice.

Ignoring CPU processing:

$$
Throughput\approx
\frac{2\,Gbps}{2}
$$

$$
\boxed{\approx1\,Gbps}
$$

The actual simplified example gets around **760 Mbps** because CPU processing time is also included.

---

# 11. General Formula

For this simplified model:

Let:

* \(S\) = packet size in bits
* \(B\) = bus bandwidth in bits/sec
* \(I\) = number of instructions
* \(F\) = CPU frequency

Then:

### Input transfer time

$$
T_{in}=\frac{S}{B}
$$

### CPU time

$$
T_{CPU}=\frac{I}{F}
$$

### Output transfer time

$$
T_{out}=\frac{S}{B}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
T_{total}=
\frac{2S}{B}+\frac{I}{F}
}
$$

And throughput:

$$
\boxed{
Throughput=\frac{S}{T_{total}}
}
$$

where \(S\) is in **bits**.

---

# 12. Example Variation

Suppose:

* Bus = 4 Gbps
* Packet = 80 bytes
* CPU = 2 GHz
* Instructions = 400

Then:

$$
S=80\times8=640\ bits
$$

Transfer time:

$$
T_{transfer}=
\frac{640}{4\times10^9}
=160\,ns
$$

CPU:

$$
T_{CPU}=
\frac{400}{2\times10^9}
=200\,ns
$$

Total:

$$
160+200+160=520\,ns
$$

Throughput:

$$
\frac{640}{520\times10^{-9}}
\approx1.231\,Gbps
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{Throughput\approx1.23\,Gbps}
$$

---

# 13. Very Important Conceptual Question

### What limits the throughput?

There are potentially two bottlenecks:

### 1. Bus bottleneck

If packet movement takes too much time:

$$
T_{bus}\gg T_{CPU}
$$

then the bus limits performance.

### 2. CPU bottleneck

If packet processing takes too much time:

$$
T_{CPU}\gg T_{bus}
$$

then CPU processing becomes the bottleneck.

Therefore:

> **The slowest/most time-consuming component in the packet-processing path limits throughput.**

---

# 14. How Can We Improve Linux PC Router Performance?

Your PPT gives three important solutions.

## Solution 1 — Reduce Data Copying

Instead of transferring the entire packet repeatedly:

```text
NIC → Memory → CPU → Memory → NIC
```

we can reduce the amount of data copied.

For example:

> Copy only the packet header to memory for processing and avoid unnecessary copying of the complete packet.

This reduces bus usage.

---

# 15. Solution 2 — Parallelize Packet Processing

Use:

* Multiple CPUs
* Multiple cores
* Parallel packet processing

Instead of:

```text
Packet 1
   ↓
CPU
   ↓
Packet 2
   ↓
CPU
```

we can process multiple packets concurrently.

```text
Packet 1 → Core 1
Packet 2 → Core 2
Packet 3 → Core 3
Packet 4 → Core 4
```

This can increase packet-processing capacity.

---

# 16. Solution 3 — Faster Switching Fabric

Use efficient internal data paths/backplanes.

The goal is to avoid unnecessary contention and access delays.

```text
Slow internal path
      ↓
Lower throughput

Faster parallel paths
      ↓
Higher throughput
```

This idea leads directly into the next major topic:

> **Switching Fabrics**

---

# 17. Exam-Trap Question

### Q: If the CPU frequency is doubled, will router throughput necessarily double?

**No.**

Why?

Because CPU processing is only one component of total processing time.

Suppose:

$$
T_{total}=T_{bus}+T_{CPU}
$$

If bus transfer time dominates, making the CPU faster will provide only limited improvement.

This is an example of a **bottleneck**.

---

# 18. Another Important Scenario

Suppose:

```text
CPU processing = 20 ns
Bus transfers = 400 ns
```

Then:

$$
T_{total}=420\,ns
$$

If CPU processing becomes:

$$
20\rightarrow10\,ns
$$

new total:

$$
410\,ns
$$

Only a small improvement occurs.

But if we reduce bus transfer time:

$$
400\rightarrow200\,ns
$$

then:

$$
T_{total}=220\,ns
$$

Much larger improvement.

Therefore:

> **Optimize the actual bottleneck, not necessarily the fastest component.**
